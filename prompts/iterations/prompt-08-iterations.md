# Version 2:
"I'm testing ad copy for a new campaign on instagram. Here's the brief: [Product: Ananas Football 'The Ivory Coast' jersey collection. Key message: premium fashion piece, not just sportswear. Audience: 20-35, interested in fashion/streetwear, not necessarily football fans. Platform: Instagram ads]. Give me 5 short ad lines (under 40 words each) that I can A/B test against each other. Make sure each one takes a genuinely different approach — don't just reword the same angle five times. Try things like: leading with the benefit, telling a mini story, creating urgency, using social proof, or just being straightforward and simple. If there's no real testimonial, stat, or proof point in the brief, skip that angle rather than making something up.
# Output:
1. Benefit-led
   Wear football inspiration without looking like you're headed to the pitch. The Ivory Coast is premium style, built for everyday wear.
2. Mini story
   You spotted the jersey. They noticed the fit. The Ivory Coast blurs the line between football culture and modern fashion.
3. Straightforward
   The Ivory Coast: a premium jersey collection designed to be styled, not just worn on match day.
4. Identity-driven
   For people who appreciate great design—not just the game. Discover The Ivory Coast collection.
5. Exclusivity / Urgency
   Not every jersey belongs in your wardrobe. The Ivory Coast was made for the ones that do. Shop the collection now.

# What changed
The second prompt is more specific than the first. It asks for genuinely different creative approaches instead of simple rewording, suggests messaging angles such as benefit-led or storytelling, and tells the model not to invent proof points that aren't in the brief. This leads to more useful variations for A/B testing.
